# Executive Summary

The UAC Program operates in an environment where border activity, enforcement changes, and humanitarian events can quickly change the number of children requiring federal care. Historical reporting is necessary, but it does not give planners enough advance warning to scale shelters, medical staff, caseworkers, and sponsor placement operations.

This project builds a predictive analytics workflow for the HHS UAC dataset. It forecasts short-term HHS care load, estimates discharge placement demand, compares statistical and machine-learning models, and produces early-warning indicators for capacity stress.

## Key Planning Questions

- How many children are likely to be in HHS care over the next days or weeks?
- Will discharge placements be sufficient to offset transfers from CBP custody?
- When should capacity, staffing, and clinical support be scaled before stress becomes visible?

## Dashboard Capabilities

- Future care-load forecast chart with confidence intervals.
- Discharge demand forecast panel.
- Model selection and comparison across baseline, statistical, and machine-learning methods.
- Scenario adjustment to test higher or lower operational pressure.
- Capacity breach probability and surge lead-time KPIs.

## Operational Value

The workflow supports proactive decisions by translating daily reporting into forward-looking risk signals. A rising forecast, positive net pressure, or increasing breach probability can prompt earlier shelter readiness, staffing adjustments, medical resource planning, and case-management surge support.

## Recommendation

Use the dashboard as a weekly planning instrument and refresh it as new daily data arrive. Pair forecast outputs with operational intelligence, including known policy changes, seasonal migration patterns, facility constraints, and sponsor placement bottlenecks.
